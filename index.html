Step 1

Matrix Representation

▼
Concept

Representing observations as matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ and target as vector $b \in \mathbb{R}^n$.

Purpose

Linear algebra models system dynamics as $A x \approx b$; requires dense standardized coordinates.

Outcome

Constructed standardized training matrix $A$ and centered vector $b$.

$$\text{Model Formulation: } \quad A x \approx b, \quad A_{i,j} = \frac{X_{i,j} - \mu_j}{\sigma_j}, \quad b_i = y_i - \bar{y}$$

Step 2

Matrix Simplification (RREF & LU Factorization)

▼
Concept

Gaussian-Jordan elimination to RREF and LU factorization with partial row pivoting ($P A_{\text{blk}} = L U$).

Purpose

Exposes pivot positions, verifies linear equation solvability, and factors into triangular systems.

Outcome

Inspected leading $6 \times 6$ block; reconstructed $P L U = A_{\text{blk}}$ with zero residual error.

$$\text{LU Factorization with Partial Pivoting: } \quad P \cdot A_{\text{blk}} = L \cdot U \iff A_{\text{blk}} = P^T L U$$

Step 3

Structure of the Space (Rank & Nullity)

▼
Concept

Rank-Nullity Theorem: $\text{rank}(A) + \text{nullity}(A) = d$.

Purpose

Counts how many features carry genuinely distinct information versus linear redundancy.

Outcome

Determined column space dimension and redundancy degree.

$$\text{Fundamental Subspaces Dimension: } \quad \dim(\mathcal{C}(A)) = \text{rank}(A), \quad \dim(\mathcal{N}(A)) = d - \text{rank}(A)$$
Step 4

Remove Redundancy (Linear Independence & Basis Selection)

▼
Concept

Basis extraction via column-pivoted QR (greedy Gram-Schmidt with maximum norm pivoting).

Purpose

Discards collinear columns to ensure the remaining basis matrix $B$ has full column rank.

Outcome

Selected independent columns; pruned redundant feature combinations.

$$\text{Basis Matrix: } \quad B = A_{[:, \text{basis}]}, \quad \text{rank}(B) = \text{cols}(B) = r$$
Step 5

Orthogonalization (Gram-Schmidt Orthonormal Basis Q)

▼
Concept

Gram-Schmidt process constructing orthonormal vectors $q_j$ such that $q_i^T q_j = \delta_{ij}$.

Purpose

Eliminates geometric skew and numerical collinearity, simplifying orthogonal projection.

Outcome

Formed matrix $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r}$; verified $Q^T Q = I_r$ to machine precision.

$$\text{Gram-Schmidt: } \quad v_j = b_j - \sum_{i=1}^{j-1} (q_i^T b_j) q_i, \quad q_j = \frac{v_j}{\|v_j\|_2}, \quad Q^T Q = I_r$$
First Rows Preview of Orthonormal Matrix $Q$ Check: $Q^T Q = I$
Step 6

Orthogonal Projection & Residual Analysis

▼
Concept

Orthogonal projection of $b$ onto column space $\mathcal{C}(B)$: $\hat{b} = Q Q^T b$.

Purpose

When $b \notin \mathcal{C}(B)$, finds the unique closest point; the error is perpendicular to every column.

Outcome

Decomposed $b = \hat{b} + e$; confirmed $B^T e \approx 0$ and $\|b\|^2 = \|\hat{b}\|^2 + \|e\|^2$.

$$\text{Orthogonal Decomposition: } \quad p = Q (Q^T b), \quad e = b - p, \quad B^T e = 0, \quad \|b\|_2^2 = \|p\|_2^2 + \|e\|_2^2$$
Step 7

Least Squares Solution (QR vs Normal Equations)

▼
Concept

Solving $R x = Q^T b$ vs $(B^T B) x = B^T b$ to find optimal regression coefficients.

Purpose

QR avoids squaring condition number $\kappa(B)$; both yield the same optimal parameter vector $x$.

Outcome

Fitted optimal weights; validated equality with normal equations solution.

$$\text{Least Squares Estimator: } \quad x = (B^T B)^{-1} B^T b = R^{-1} Q^T b, \quad \text{where } B = Q R$$

Step 8

Eigen-Decomposition (Covariance & Jacobi Algorithm)

▼
Concept

Spectral decomposition of sample covariance $C = V \Lambda V^T$ via cyclic Jacobi rotations.

Purpose

Discovers principal axes of maximum variance and uncovers latent feature correlations.

Outcome

Calculated eigenvalues and eigenvectors; confirmed $C v_i = \lambda_i v_i$ and $\sum \lambda_i = \text{tr}(C)$.

$$\text{Spectral Theorem: } \quad C = \frac{1}{n-1} B^T B = V \Lambda V^T, \quad C v_i = \lambda_i v_i, \quad \sum_{i=1}^r \lambda_i = \text{tr}(C)$$
Step 9

Diagonalization / PCA (Dimensionality Reduction)

▼
Concept

Selecting $k$ leading eigenvectors $W = [v_1, \dots, v_k]$ to capture $\ge$ target variance.

Purpose

Compresses the data matrix $Z = B W$, filtering out weak noise directions.

Outcome

Projected data into lower-dimensional orthogonal subspace.

$$\text{PCA Projection: } \quad Z = B \cdot W, \quad W \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k}, \quad Z \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}$$
Step 10

Final Output (Full Model vs Reduced Model vs Baseline)

▼
Concept

Fitting regression on compressed representation $Z$ and evaluating out-of-sample generalization.

Purpose

Assesses trade-off between dimensionality reduction and predictive fidelity on test data.

Outcome

Quantified RMSE delta across Full, PCA-reduced, and trivial baseline models.

$$\text{Reduced Model: } \quad x_Z = (Z^T Z)^{-1} Z^T b, \quad \hat{y}_{\text{test}} = Z_{\text{test}} x_Z + \bar{y}$$

Critical Limitations to State During Oral Viva

  • Linearity Assumption: The pipeline model is strictly linear ($A x \approx b$). It cannot capture nonlinear interactions without polynomial or kernel expansions.
  • Numeric Target Requirement: Requires a continuous numeric target $b$; not suitable for multi-class classification without logistic/softmax extensions.
  • Unsupervised Nature of PCA: PCA selects directions of maximum feature variance without looking at the target $b$. If the predictive signal lies along low-variance directions, PCA compression can harm test RMSE.
  • Sensitivity to Outliers: Least squares minimizes squared Euclidean distance ($\ell_2$ norm), making weights and eigenvalues sensitive to extreme outliers.
